10Race execution

No separate running warm-up is added on top of the sixteen. Use brisk walking, ankle/hip/glute activation and non-running mobility. Any actual running pickup or warm-up recorded by Garmin counts toward the 16.00-mile combined total.

Segment Power target Pace picture Competitive instruction
Start–0.5 mi 375–385 W approximately 7:40–8:05/mi Accept being passed; the course rises early.
0.5–1.5 mi 380–390 W approximately 7:20–7:40/mi Settle in a compact group and run tangents without weaving.
1.5–2.15 mi / Holly 385–395 W; brief ≤405 W approximately 7:50–8:40/mi Short stride, quick cadence; do not follow Scott above the ceiling.
2.15–3.3 mi 380–390 W approximately 7:20–7:45/mi Recover over the crest; take water at about 2.47 if wanted.
3.3–4.5 mi 385–395 W; brief ≤405 W approximately 7:40–8:20/mi Hold effort through the return climb; crest smoothly.
4.5–5.15 mi / Oceanview power may fall below 375 W approximately 6:45–7:15/mi Quick feet, slight forward lean, no braking and no forced power.
5.15–5.75 mi 385–395 W; brief ≤405 W approximately 7:35–8:20/mi Re-engage after the low point; use Rankin water for the extension.
5.75 mi–finish 385–395 W only if Green approximately 6:55–7:25/mi as the course falls Let downhill speed come; no sprint, even for a placing.

Race cue: Tall hips, elbows back, quiet feet. Relax jaw and shoulders; hands in lane.

Line up about three to five rows back, near the 7:20–7:40/mi group. Do not race the younger overall leaders. If Scott establishes a gap at effort above the guardrail, let him go. The best chance to beat him is even execution: remain controlled over the first Holly climb, avoid braking on Oceanview, and see whether the gap closes after mile five without exceeding the plan.

Garmin screen: three-second power, lap-average power, lap pace and HR. Keep one-mile auto-lap. Read lap pace as the accumulated performance signal and three-second/lap-average power as the terrain-control signal. Do not use narrow pace alerts. Save the race separately at the finish, note the recorded distance, then set the extension so all recorded running totals 16.00 miles.

11Saturday readiness gate

Friday is complete rest: no run, strides, strength, plyometrics or mileage compensation.

Green — complete the full 16:

  • at least approximately 6.5–7 hours sleep and subjectively restored;
  • HRV meaningfully rebounded toward Aaron's normal low-to-mid 40s rather than remaining below 40;
  • resting HR no more than approximately 5 bpm above normal;
  • Body Battery meaningfully recharged, preferably around 60 or higher;
  • normal gait and stairs; no sharp or escalating foot, calf, Achilles, hip, adductor or groin symptoms;
  • ordinary soreness only.

Yellow — controlled race and shorten to 12–14 total: recovery improves but remains incomplete; legs are heavy or sleep/HRV/Body Battery remain marginal; symptoms are 0–2/10 and gait is normal.

Red — no hard race and no long extension: another severely short night, sharp/increasing pain, gait change, illness, dizziness or abnormal fatigue. One favorable wearable metric does not override the composite.

12Transition, fuel and hydration

At the finish:

  1. Save the race and note its recorded distance.
  2. Clear the chute without lingering.
  3. Take staged carbohydrate, fluid and sodium.
  4. Restart within about ten minutes.
  5. Extension distance = 16.00 minus all Garmin-recorded running already completed.

Use 290–305 W preferred in the warming conditions, approximately 10:00–10:45/mi, with 315 W as the upper working limit and 325 W as the absolute ceiling. No fast finish. Stop or shorten if power must exceed 325 W to preserve ordinary easy pace, HR rises disproportionately despite slowing, downhill quadriceps damage changes mechanics, pain rises, gait changes, dizziness appears or cooling becomes ineffective.

  • Familiar soy-free, low-fiber breakfast around 06:00–06:15 with roughly 60–90 g carbohydrate.
  • About 12–16 oz familiar sodium-containing fluid by 07:45; sip afterward rather than overdrink.
  • Familiar pre-start carbohydrate if already rehearsed.
  • Water stops are cups and water only. A quick sip near 2.47 miles is optional; the Rankin pass near 5.29 miles is useful before the extension.
  • Take carbohydrate immediately after the finish and continue the established 60–75 g/hour range only as tolerated, including drink carbohydrate. A practical pattern is a familiar gel before the start, one at the finish, then about every 30 minutes in the extension.
  • Carry a familiar handheld/soft flask or arrange a trusted handoff. Do not rely on a backpack or unattended bag: there is no bag check and backpacks are prohibited.
  • No new nutrition products.

15Runner reports and atmosphere

A 2012 participant described the course as hilly from start to finish, with a severe last-mile climb followed by a welcome downhill finish; the same report praised unusually strong neighborhood cheering, extra water and hoses. A 2016 local report described 215 finishers in hot, humid conditions and quoted runners emphasizing conservative pacing, fans, sprinklers and volunteer support.

These are useful atmosphere signals, not current operational promises. Expect a supportive neighborhood race and enjoy the local energy, but rely only on the two water-only stations confirmed in the 2026 organizer email.

16Awards and post-race

Custom sweatshirts go to the first three male and female overall, first male and female Brielle residents, first firefighter, law-enforcement officer and EMT, and top three male and female finishers in each ten-year age group. There are no duplicate awards. Select winners receive gift cards, and there is one grand-prize raffle.

Awards begin directly after the race, which conflicts with the ten-minute extension restart. Ask at pickup whether a podium sweatshirt can be held, or arrange a trusted person to monitor results and collect it. Waiting for posted results or the ceremony would turn the day into race plus later mileage rather than the intended near-continuous sixteen.

After the extension, take carbohydrate, protein, fluids and sodium before using the happy-hour wristband. Post-race food and Brielle Day vendors are part of the enjoyment plan; they are not a reason to lengthen the transition or cut recovery nutrition.
